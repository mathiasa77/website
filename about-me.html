A Brief Biography

At an early age I found the notion of life and existence very peculiar. Without getting too existential, I believe science and academia to be our most trusted and tested methods of discovering the world around us. I was fortunate enough to grow up in Mountain View, CA, down the street from NASA Ames Research Center. Going to school with not only the sons and daughters of Silicon Valley engineers, but of NASA engineers, nurtured an environment that valued education and STEM. A gift I would only come to appreciate as an adult.

As a high school student at Mountain View High School I took advantage of the wide variety of unique classes offered like Engineering Technology I and II and Physics AP. Frankly, I spent the majority of time on my engineering studies more than anything else. Although having somewhat of a one track mind at the time, I knew college was an endaevor I wanted to embark on. At the time of my graduation in 2017 I was savagely craving my independence, inevitably deciding to move to San Diego to begin my college career.

It was in my second year of community college where I decided I wanted to investigate the mechanisms of nature further; declaring a major in physics. My fascination with the natural world only grew when I was accepted and transferred into the University of California, Berkeley (one of the best days of my life I might add). Here I was led on a wild-ride through the macro and quantum world, satiating mathematical appetite.

Aside from challenging coursework, once I become a member of a rocket team it was abundantly clear I wanted to position my career in a way where my time would be spent on space technology.

A central goal of mine is to further our understanding of space beyond this pale blue dot. I believe that through the research and development of space technologies we can further extend human presence in space, efforts of which I plan to contribute greatly throughout my life.

I had the privilege of taking a 3-month sabbatical traveling around Europe post graduation from U.C. Berkeley. Currently, I continue to pursue a career in Aerospace Engineering, and I will be pursuing a Ph.D in the future.

Early Life in Aquatics

In my early years I had a relatively active presence in my community. Around the age of 13 I started volunteering as a swim instructor at the City of Mountain View Eagle Pool, an experience that would lead to a part-time job as a Lifeguard when I turned 15. Fast forward 4 years and here I found myself working as a Head Lifeguard; managing a staff of 60 employees, resolving conflicts, and taking ownership of being a keyholder.

There are plenty of reasons why lifeguarding is a great job but a few really stood out to me. As a young person, being given such a large responsibility, ensuring the safety of others, didn't weigh lightly on me. Besides having to maintain numerous CPR, advanced first-aid, and other related certifications, I had the responsibility of organizing staff training, maintaining pool chemistry, and ensuring a safe environment.

It also turns out lifeguarding was the perfect job for a student, something I found out in high school. I carried on lifeguarding throughout my community college experience at the La Jolla Family YMCA in San Diego, working 20 hours a week and quickly getting promoted to Head Lifeguard once again. By the time I was ready to transfer to a university I had been working as a Lifeguard for about 7 years, and I was sad to say goodbye to the industry. All in all, it is a central contributor to the adult I am today, and I am immensely grateful for all the values and lessons it has taught me throughout the years.
